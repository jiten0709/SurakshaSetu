# Income-tax Act 2025: Schedule II, life insurance proceeds (DUMMY excerpt)

## 1. Exemption for sums received under a life insurance policy

DUMMY: Any sum received under a life insurance policy, including the sum allocated by way of bonus on the policy, shall not be included in the total income of the recipient for any tax year, subject to the conditions in the following paragraphs. The exemption applies to maturity benefits, survival benefits, returns of premium on maturity and death benefits alike, unless a condition in this Schedule excludes them. The exemption is available whether the total income of the recipient is computed under section 202 or under the old regime. This paragraph corresponds to section 10(10D) of the Income-tax Act 1961. The exemption does not apply to any sum received under a keyman insurance policy or under a policy taken by an employer for an employee in a way that makes the sum taxable as salary.

## 2. Premium to sum assured condition

DUMMY: The exemption in paragraph 1 does not apply to a sum received under a policy issued on or after the first day of April 2012 if the premium payable in any year during the term of the policy exceeds ten percent of the actual capital sum assured, or twenty percent for a policy issued between the first day of April 2003 and the thirty-first day of March 2012. For a policy on the life of a person with a disability or a person suffering from a specified disease, issued on or after the first day of April 2013, the limit is fifteen percent. The condition is tested for every year of the policy term, so a policy whose premium in any single year exceeds the limit loses the exemption for its maturity proceeds.

## 3. Unit linked policies with high premium

DUMMY: The exemption does not apply to any sum received under a unit linked insurance policy issued on or after the first day of February 2021 if the premium payable for any of the previous years during the term of the policy, or the aggregate premium payable for all unit linked policies issued to the assessee on or after that date, exceeds two lakh and fifty thousand rupees. Where the exemption is lost, the sum received in excess of the premium paid is chargeable to tax as capital gains, in the manner that the Act provides for equity-oriented funds.

## 4. Non-linked policies with high premium

DUMMY: The exemption does not apply to any sum received under a life insurance policy, other than a unit linked insurance policy, issued on or after the first day of April 2023, if the premium payable for any year during the term of the policy exceeds five lakh rupees, or if the aggregate premium payable for all such policies issued to the assessee on or after that date exceeds five lakh rupees in any year. Where the exemption is lost under this paragraph, the sum received in excess of the aggregate premium paid, to the extent it has not been allowed as a deduction, is chargeable to tax as income from other sources in the year of receipt.

## 5. Death benefit always exempt

DUMMY: Notwithstanding paragraphs 2, 3 and 4, any sum received on the death of a person under a life insurance policy is always excluded from the total income of the recipient, whatever the premium, the sum assured or the date of issue of the policy. The death benefit of a term insurance policy, of a return of premium policy and of a savings policy is therefore not taxable in the hands of the nominee or the legal heir. The conditions on premium and aggregate premium affect only sums received otherwise than on death, such as maturity benefits, survival benefits, returns of premium and surrender values.

## 6. Tax deducted at source on taxable proceeds

DUMMY: Where a sum paid under a life insurance policy, including bonus, is not excluded from total income under this Schedule, the insurer shall deduct income-tax at source at the rate of two percent on the income comprised in the payment, being the amount paid less the aggregate premium paid, as provided in the Act's provisions on deduction at source, which succeed section 194DA of the Income-tax Act 1961. No deduction at source is made where the amount paid in the tax year is less than one hundred thousand rupees in aggregate. The recipient remains liable for any further tax on the income under the rates that apply to them.
