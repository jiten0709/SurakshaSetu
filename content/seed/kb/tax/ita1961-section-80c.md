# Income-tax Act 1961: Section 80C (DUMMY excerpt, superseded)

## 80C(1) Deduction up to one lakh fifty thousand rupees

DUMMY: In computing the total income of an assessee, being an individual or a Hindu undivided family, there shall be deducted the whole of the amount paid or deposited in the previous year, being the aggregate of the sums referred to in sub-section (2), as does not exceed one lakh and fifty thousand rupees. This section applies to the assessment years up to 2026-27, that is, to income of the previous years up to 2025-26. From the tax year 2026-27, the corresponding provision is section 123 of the Income-tax Act 2025 read with Schedule XV. A deduction under this section is not available to an assessee who pays tax under the new regime of section 115BAC.

## 80C(2) Payments that qualify

DUMMY: The sums referred to in sub-section (1) include any payment by an individual to effect or keep in force an insurance on the life of the individual, the spouse or any child, and by a Hindu undivided family on the life of any member; any payment under a contract for a deferred annuity; contributions to a provident fund or the public provident fund; subscription to national savings certificates and the senior citizens' savings scheme; contribution to a unit linked insurance plan; tuition fees for up to two children; repayment of a housing loan principal; and investment in notified equity-linked savings schemes and five-year bank term deposits. A premium on the life of a parent or other relative does not qualify.

## 80C(3) and 80C(3A) Premium limit as a percentage of sum assured

DUMMY: Under sub-section (3), the deduction for a life insurance premium on a policy issued on or before the thirty-first day of March 2012 is limited to twenty percent of the actual capital sum assured. Under sub-section (3A), for a policy issued on or after the first day of April 2012, the deduction is limited to ten percent of the actual capital sum assured, and for a policy on the life of a person with a disability referred to in section 80U or suffering from a disease specified under section 80DDB, issued on or after the first day of April 2013, to fifteen percent. The excess premium is not deductible even within the overall limit.

## 80C(5) Reversal of deduction on termination within two years

DUMMY: Where the assessee, in any previous year, terminates a contract of life insurance by notice or by not paying the premium, before premiums have been paid for two years, or in the case of a single premium policy terminates it within two years after the date of commencement of insurance, no deduction is allowed for the premium paid in that year, and the aggregate of the deductions allowed in earlier years for that policy is deemed to be the income of that previous year and taxed accordingly. For a unit linked insurance plan, the corresponding period is five years of contributions.

## 80CCE Aggregate limit across sections

DUMMY: Section 80CCE provides that the aggregate amount of deductions under section 80C, section 80CCC and sub-section (1) of section 80CCD shall not exceed one lakh and fifty thousand rupees. An individual who pays life insurance premiums, contributes to a pension plan of an insurer and contributes to the national pension system therefore has one combined limit for these three sections. The additional deduction of up to fifty thousand rupees for contributions to the national pension system under section 80CCD(1B) is outside this aggregate limit. In the Income-tax Act 2025, the single aggregate limit of Schedule XV read with section 123 replaces section 80CCE.

## 80C and the new regime of section 115BAC

DUMMY: Under section 115BAC, which from the assessment year 2024-25 became the default tax regime for individuals and Hindu undivided families, an assessee paying tax at the concessional rates is not allowed a deduction under section 80C, 80CCC or most other deductions of Chapter VI-A. An assessee who wanted the deduction for life insurance premium had to opt out of section 115BAC for that year. For individuals without business income, the choice could be made each year in the return of income. From the tax year 2026-27, section 202 of the Income-tax Act 2025 succeeds section 115BAC, and the same rule applies to section 123.
