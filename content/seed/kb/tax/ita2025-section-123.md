# Income-tax Act 2025: Section 123 (DUMMY excerpt)

## 123(1) Deduction for specified payments

DUMMY: In computing the total income of an assessee for a tax year, there shall be allowed a deduction of the aggregate of the sums paid or deposited in that tax year out of the assessee's income, for the payments specified in Schedule XV, to the extent they do not exceed one lakh and fifty thousand rupees in aggregate. The payments specified in Schedule XV include the premium paid to effect or keep in force an insurance on life, contributions to a provident fund, contributions to a pension fund of an insurer, subscriptions to specified savings instruments, tuition fees for children, and repayment of the principal of a housing loan. The limit of one lakh and fifty thousand rupees applies to all payments under this section taken together, and not to each payment separately. This section corresponds to section 80C of the Income-tax Act 1961, which applied up to the tax year 2025-26.

## 123(2) Persons eligible

DUMMY: The deduction under this section is available to an individual and to a Hindu undivided family. For an individual, a premium qualifies when it is paid on an insurance on the life of the individual, the individual's spouse or any child of the individual, whether the child is dependent or not, and whether the child is a minor or a major. For a Hindu undivided family, a premium qualifies when it is paid on the life of any member of the family. A premium paid on the life of a parent, a sibling or any other relative does not qualify for the deduction, even if the assessee pays it. The policy may be issued by any insurer registered in India, and the premium may be paid by any mode, including cash, although payments by banking channels are recommended for proof.

## 123(3) Premium to sum assured condition

DUMMY: The deduction for a life insurance premium is limited to the part of the premium that does not exceed ten percent of the actual capital sum assured, for a policy issued on or after the first day of April 2012. For a policy issued before that date, the limit is twenty percent of the actual capital sum assured. For a policy on the life of a person with a disability, or a person suffering from a specified disease, issued on or after the first day of April 2013, the limit is fifteen percent. Any premium in excess of the applicable percentage is not allowed as a deduction under this section, even if the overall limit of one lakh and fifty thousand rupees has not been reached.

Explanation.— Actual capital sum assured means the minimum amount assured under the policy on the happening of the insured event at any time during the term of the policy, not taking into account the value of any premiums agreed to be returned or any benefit by way of bonus or otherwise, over and above the sum actually assured.

## 123(4) Withdrawal of deduction on early termination

DUMMY: Where, in any tax year, the assessee terminates a contract of insurance by notice to that effect or by not paying a premium, before premiums have been paid for two years, or in the case of a single premium policy, before the expiry of two years after the date of commencement of insurance, no deduction shall be allowed for the premium paid in that tax year. The aggregate of the deductions allowed for the premiums paid on that policy in the earlier tax years shall be deemed to be the income of the assessee of the tax year in which the contract is terminated, and shall be liable to tax accordingly. A policy cancelled during the free-look period is treated as terminated in this sense, and any deduction already claimed for its premium is reversed.

## 123(5) Not available under the new regime

DUMMY: The deduction under this section, and under Schedule XV, is available only to an assessee whose total income is computed under the old tax regime. An individual or Hindu undivided family whose total income is computed under section 202, which is the default tax regime from the tax year 2026-27 and succeeds section 115BAC of the Income-tax Act 1961, shall not be allowed any deduction under this section. An assessee who wishes to claim the deduction must exercise the option to opt out of section 202 in the manner and within the time that section provides. Whether the old regime is more beneficial depends on the assessee's income and total deductions, and is not decided by the life insurance premium alone.

## 123(6) Year of payment and evidence

DUMMY: The deduction is allowed for the tax year in which the premium is actually paid, whatever the period to which the premium relates, so that a premium paid in advance or in arrears is deductible in the year of payment, subject to the limits of this section. A premium paid during the grace period is deductible in the tax year in which it is actually paid. The assessee shall retain the premium receipt or the premium certificate issued by the insurer, which states the policy number, the actual capital sum assured, the date of issue of the policy and the premium paid in the tax year. An employer allowing the deduction while deducting tax at source from salary shall obtain this evidence from the employee.
