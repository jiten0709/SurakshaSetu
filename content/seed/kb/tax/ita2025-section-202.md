# Income-tax Act 2025: Section 202 (DUMMY excerpt)

## 202(1) The default tax regime

DUMMY: The income-tax payable on the total income of an individual or a Hindu undivided family for a tax year shall be computed at the rates specified in this section, which is the default tax regime, unless the assessee opts out of it in the manner provided in sub-section (4). This section succeeds section 115BAC of the Income-tax Act 1961, under which the new tax regime had become the default from the assessment year 2024-25. The rates under this section are lower than the rates under the old tax regime, in exchange for the assessee giving up most deductions and exemptions, including the deduction for life insurance premium under section 123 and Schedule XV.

## 202(2) Deductions not allowed under the default regime

DUMMY: An assessee whose income is computed under this section shall not be allowed any deduction under section 123 or Schedule XV, including the deduction for life insurance premium, contributions to a provident fund, tuition fees and housing loan principal. The deductions for health insurance premium and for interest on a housing loan for a self-occupied property are also not allowed. The standard deduction from salary income and the deduction for the employer's contribution to the national pension system continue to be allowed. Exemptions for sums received under a life insurance policy, as provided in Schedule II, are not affected by the choice of regime and remain available under both regimes, subject to their own conditions.

## 202(3) Rebate and rates

DUMMY: The rates of income-tax under this section, and the rebate that reduces the tax payable to nil for a resident individual whose total income does not exceed the limit specified in the Finance Act for the tax year, are prescribed annually in the Finance Act. Because the rates and the rebate limit change from year to year, any statement about whether the default regime or the old regime is more beneficial for a particular assessee depends on the assessee's income, deductions and the Finance Act of that year, and shall not be made without that information.

## 202(4) Opting out of the default regime

DUMMY: An individual or Hindu undivided family not having income from business or profession may opt out of this section for any tax year by exercising the option in the return of income furnished on or before the due date for that tax year, and may choose differently in each tax year. An assessee having income from business or profession may opt out only once by filing the prescribed form before the due date, and may re-enter the default regime only once thereafter. An assessee who opts out has total income computed under the old regime, with its deductions, including section 123, and its rates.
