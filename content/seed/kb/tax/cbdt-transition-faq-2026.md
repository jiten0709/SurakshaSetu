# CBDT Transition FAQs on the Income-tax Act 2025 (DUMMY)

## 1. Which Act applies to which year

DUMMY: The Income-tax Act 2025 came into force on the first day of April 2026 and applies to the tax year 2026-27 and every later tax year. Income of the financial year 2025-26 and earlier years continues to be assessed under the Income-tax Act 1961, including returns filed, assessments made and refunds issued after the first day of April 2026. The 2025 Act uses a single term, tax year, in place of the previous year and assessment year of the 1961 Act, so income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 belongs to the tax year 2026-27. A question about a deduction claimed for premiums paid in 2025-26 is answered under the 1961 Act, and a question about premiums paid from April 2026 under the 2025 Act.

## 2. What happened to section 80C

DUMMY: Section 80C of the 1961 Act has no section with the same number in the 2025 Act. Its deduction continues in section 123 of the 2025 Act, and the list of qualifying payments, including life insurance premium, is in Schedule XV. The aggregate limit remains one lakh and fifty thousand rupees, and the premium to sum assured conditions of ten, fifteen and twenty percent continue for policies according to their date of issue. A life insurance premium paid in the tax year 2026-27 is claimed under section 123 read with paragraph 1 of Schedule XV, and not under section 80C.

## 3. Which regime allows the premium deduction

DUMMY: The deduction for life insurance premium under section 123 is available only under the old regime. Section 202 of the 2025 Act, which succeeds section 115BAC of the 1961 Act, is the default regime, and an assessee under section 202 cannot claim section 123. An individual without business income who wants the deduction must opt out of section 202 in the return of income for that tax year. The tax-free status of the death benefit and, subject to conditions, of maturity proceeds does not depend on the regime chosen.

## 4. Policies bought before April 2026

DUMMY: A policy issued before the first day of April 2026 does not need to be changed because of the new Act. Premiums paid on it from the tax year 2026-27 qualify under section 123 on the same conditions as before, with the premium limit decided by the policy's date of issue. The exemption of its maturity proceeds is decided under Schedule II of the 2025 Act using the same premium and aggregate premium conditions that applied under section 10(10D), including the five lakh rupees limit for non-linked policies issued from the first day of April 2023. The death benefit remains exempt in all cases.

## 5. References to old sections in policy documents

DUMMY: Policy documents, brochures and premium certificates issued before the first day of April 2026 refer to sections 80C, 80CCC, 10(10D) and 194DA of the 1961 Act. For the tax year 2026-27 and later years, such a reference is read as a reference to the corresponding provision of the 2025 Act: section 123 and Schedule XV for 80C and 80CCC, Schedule II for 10(10D), and the corresponding provision on deduction at source for 194DA. Insurers are not required to reissue old documents, but new documents and certificates must cite the 2025 Act.

## 6. Tax statements by insurers and intermediaries

DUMMY: An insurer or intermediary may describe the general tax treatment of a life insurance product, but must state that tax benefits are subject to the conditions of the law in force and may change, and must not compute the tax payable or the tax saved by a particular customer without the information needed to do so. A statement that a premium is tax-deductible must mention that the deduction is available only under the old regime and within the aggregate limit of one lakh and fifty thousand rupees. A customer who needs a personal computation should be directed to a tax advisor.
