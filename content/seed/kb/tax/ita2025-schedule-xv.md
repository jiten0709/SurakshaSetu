# Income-tax Act 2025: Schedule XV (DUMMY excerpt)

## 1. Life insurance premium

DUMMY: Paragraph 1 of Schedule XV specifies, for the purposes of section 123, any sum paid to effect or to keep in force an insurance on the life of the individual, the individual's spouse or any child of the individual, or, in the case of a Hindu undivided family, of any member of the family. The sum includes premium paid on a term insurance policy, an endowment policy, a return of premium policy or a whole life policy, and the premium for a rider attached to such a policy where the rider premium is part of the premium for insurance on life. The sum does not include taxes charged on the premium, or any amount paid for a health insurance policy, which is dealt with separately under the Act. The deduction for this paragraph is subject to the premium to sum assured condition in section 123(3).

## 2. Deferred annuity and pension plans of insurers

DUMMY: Paragraph 2 specifies any sum paid under a contract for a deferred annuity on the life of the individual, the spouse or any child, not being a contract that gives the individual the option to receive the annuity in cash in lieu of payment of the annuity. Paragraph 3 specifies contributions to a pension fund of an insurer for receiving a pension, which corresponds to section 80CCC of the Income-tax Act 1961. The amount standing to the credit of the assessee under such a pension plan, when surrendered or received as pension, including any interest or bonus, is chargeable to tax in the year of receipt, to the extent a deduction was allowed for the contributions.

## 3. Unit linked insurance plans

DUMMY: Paragraph 4 specifies contributions to a unit linked insurance plan of an insurer, for the individual, the spouse or any child, or for a member of a Hindu undivided family. The premium to sum assured condition of section 123(3) applies to a unit linked insurance plan as it applies to any other life insurance policy. Where the assessee terminates participation in a unit linked insurance plan before contributions have been paid for five years, the deduction allowed in earlier tax years is withdrawn and taxed as income of the year of termination, in the same way as under section 123(4) for other policies.

## 4. Provident fund and savings instruments

DUMMY: Paragraphs 5 to 9 specify contributions to a statutory or recognised provident fund and to the public provident fund, subscriptions to the national savings certificates and to the senior citizens' savings scheme, deposits in a five-year term deposit with a scheduled bank, and subscriptions to equity-linked savings schemes of mutual funds as notified. Each of these payments qualifies only if it is made by the assessee in the tax year out of income, and each is subject to the conditions of the relevant scheme, including any lock-in period. These payments share the aggregate limit of one lakh and fifty thousand rupees with the life insurance premium under paragraph 1.

## 5. Tuition fees and housing loan repayment

DUMMY: Paragraph 10 specifies tuition fees, excluding any development fees, donation or similar payment, paid at the time of admission or afterwards to any university, college, school or other educational institution situated in India, for the full-time education of up to two children of the individual. Paragraph 11 specifies the repayment of the principal amount of a loan taken from a bank or specified lender to purchase or construct a residential house, together with stamp duty and registration fees on its transfer. These payments share the aggregate limit with every other payment in this Schedule.

## 6. Aggregate limit and interaction with other deductions

DUMMY: The aggregate amount of deduction for all payments specified in this Schedule shall not exceed one lakh and fifty thousand rupees in a tax year. This single aggregate limit replaces the combined limit that section 80CCE of the Income-tax Act 1961 placed on sections 80C, 80CCC and 80CCD(1). An additional deduction for contribution to the national pension system, up to fifty thousand rupees, is allowed separately and is not part of this aggregate limit. The deductions under this Schedule are available only under the old tax regime, as provided in section 123(5).

## 7. Correspondence with the Income-tax Act 1961

DUMMY: For the purpose of reading documents, certificates and policy literature that refer to the Income-tax Act 1961, paragraph 1 of this Schedule corresponds to clauses of section 80C relating to life insurance premium, paragraph 3 corresponds to section 80CCC, and paragraph 6 of this Schedule read with section 123(1) corresponds to section 80CCE. A reference to section 80C in a policy document issued before the first day of April 2026 shall, for the tax year 2026-27 and later tax years, be read as a reference to section 123 and this Schedule. Premium certificates issued by insurers from the tax year 2026-27 cite section 123 and Schedule XV.
