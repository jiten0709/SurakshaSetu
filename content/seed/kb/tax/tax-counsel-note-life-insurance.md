# Tax counsel note: life insurance under the Income-tax Act 2025 (DUMMY)

## 1. Deduction for term insurance premium

DUMMY: A premium paid on a term insurance plan, such as Suraksha Term Shield, on the life of the customer, the spouse or a child, qualifies for deduction under section 123 read with Schedule XV from the tax year 2026-27, and under section 80C for the tax year 2025-26 and earlier. The deduction is limited to the premium that does not exceed ten percent of the actual capital sum assured for policies issued after March 2012, which a term plan almost always meets because its premium is a small fraction of the cover. The deduction shares the aggregate limit of one lakh and fifty thousand rupees with provident fund, tuition fees, housing loan principal and other qualifying payments, and it is lost entirely if the customer stays in the default regime of section 202.

## 2. Return of premium plans

DUMMY: The premium of a return of premium plan, such as Suraksha Term Shield ROP, qualifies for deduction on the same basis as a term plan, subject to the premium to sum assured condition. The return of premium paid on survival to maturity is exempt under Schedule II, or section 10(10D) for earlier years, provided the premium in every year of the term stayed within ten percent of the actual capital sum assured and, for policies issued from April 2023, the aggregate annual premium on all such non-linked policies did not exceed five lakh rupees. When a return of premium plan is surrendered early, the surrender value is tested under the same conditions.

## 3. Savings plan maturity proceeds

DUMMY: The maturity benefit and guaranteed additions of a non-linked savings plan, such as Suraksha Saver Guarantee, are exempt only if the policy meets the premium to sum assured condition and, for a policy issued on or after the first day of April 2023, the premium limit of five lakh rupees per year in aggregate. If the limit is breached, the maturity proceeds in excess of the premiums paid are taxable as income from other sources, and the insurer deducts tax at source on the income component. A monthly income option does not change this test, but each instalment received is examined in the year of receipt.

## 4. What the assistant may and may not say about tax

DUMMY: The assistant may explain the general rules above with citations, may say which regime allows the premium deduction, and must show the approved tax disclosure that tax treatment depends on the law in force and can change. It must not tell a customer how much tax they will save, whether the old regime is better for them, or whether a particular maturity amount will be tax-free, because each of these needs a personal computation that depends on income and other deductions. When the customer's regime is unknown, the assistant states the regime condition and may ask which regime the customer follows, without recording the answer as a slot unless the customer confirms it.
