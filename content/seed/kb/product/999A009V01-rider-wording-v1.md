# Suraksha Critical Illness Rider: Rider Wording v1 (DUMMY)

## 1. Benefit

DUMMY: This rider, unique identification number 999A009V01, may be attached at inception to Suraksha Term Shield (999N001V02) only. If the life assured is first diagnosed during the rider term with any one of the twenty critical illnesses listed in the annexure, and survives for thirty days after the diagnosis, the insurer pays the rider sum assured as a lump sum, and the rider ends. The covered illnesses include cancer of specified severity, first heart attack of specified severity, open chest coronary artery bypass graft, stroke resulting in permanent symptoms, kidney failure requiring regular dialysis, major organ or bone marrow transplant, and permanent paralysis of limbs, each as defined in the annexure. The benefit is paid once only, whatever the number of illnesses diagnosed. Payment of the rider benefit does not reduce the base sum assured on death, and the base policy continues. The rider sum assured cannot exceed the base sum assured. The rider sum assured is chosen at inception and cannot be increased later. The rider premium depends on age, tobacco use, the rider term and the rider sum assured. Diagnosis must be made by a specialist registered medical practitioner in India and supported by the investigation reports named in the definition. The benefit can be used in any way the life assured chooses, such as treatment, recovery or replacing lost income.

## 2. Exclusions

DUMMY: No benefit is paid for a critical illness caused directly or indirectly by: a pre-existing disease, meaning any condition for which the life assured had signs or symptoms, was diagnosed or received treatment within forty-eight months before the start or revival of the rider, unless disclosed and accepted; intentional self-inflicted injury or attempted suicide; the abuse of alcohol or drugs; HIV or AIDS; congenital conditions; war, invasion, civil war or rebellion; participation in a criminal act; nuclear contamination; or failure to seek or follow medical advice. Specific illnesses carry additional exclusions in their definitions; for example, early-stage cancers, carcinoma in situ and skin cancers other than malignant melanoma are not covered under the cancer definition. Exclusions of the base policy continue to apply to its own benefits. A condition is treated as pre-existing even if it was not diagnosed, where the life assured had symptoms that would have led a reasonable person to seek treatment. An illness caused by a pre-existing condition that was disclosed and accepted without exclusion is covered after the waiting period. Cosmetic and experimental treatments are not covered events, and the insurer gives written reasons with the definition relied on whenever it declines a claim.

## 3. Waiting periods and policy conditions

DUMMY: This section sets out when the rider cover begins to respond, and the free-look, grace and revival conditions that apply to the rider.

### 3.1 Waiting and survival periods

DUMMY: A waiting period of ninety days applies from the start of the rider, and again from the date of any revival. No benefit is paid for a critical illness whose signs or symptoms first appear, or which is first diagnosed, during the waiting period, and the rider premium paid is refunded if such an illness makes the rider end. The waiting period does not apply to a critical illness caused by an accident that happens after the rider starts. After the diagnosis, the life assured must survive for a survival period of thirty days; if the life assured dies within the survival period, no rider benefit is paid, but the base death benefit is payable. The date of diagnosis is the date of the specialist's report that confirms the illness according to its definition.

### 3.2 Free-look, grace and revival

DUMMY: The rider follows the policy conditions of Suraksha Term Shield, with the changes set out below.

#### 3.2.1 Free-look

DUMMY: A free-look cancellation of the base policy within thirty days of receipt cancels this rider, and the rider premium is refunded on the same basis as the base premium.

#### 3.2.2 Grace period

DUMMY: The rider premium is paid with the base premium and has the same grace period of thirty days, or fifteen days for monthly premiums; a diagnosis during the grace period is covered once the overdue premium is paid.

#### 3.2.3 Revival

DUMMY: The rider can be revived only with the base policy, within five years of the first unpaid premium, on evidence of good health. The ninety-day waiting period applies again from revival.

## 4. Surrender, termination and claims

DUMMY: This section sets out when the rider ends, what is payable when it ends, and how to make a claim under it.

### 4.1 Surrender and termination

DUMMY: This rider has no surrender value and no paid-up value. It ends on the earliest of: payment of the rider benefit; the end of the rider term; the death of the life assured; the lapse, surrender, free-look cancellation or paid-up conversion of the base policy; or the policyholder's written request to remove it from the next premium due date. A critical illness diagnosed after the rider has ended is not covered, even if the base policy continues. The premium for this rider, together with any other health-related rider on the same policy, cannot exceed one hundred percent of the base premium. If the waiver of premium rider is also attached and waives premiums, the premiums of this rider are waived too.

### 4.2 Claims

DUMMY: A claim is made through any branch, the website, the app or the call centre after the survival period of thirty days, with the claim form, the diagnosis report of a specialist registered medical practitioner confirming the illness according to its definition, the supporting investigation reports such as histopathology, angiography or imaging, the hospital discharge summary, and proof of identity. The insurer may ask for an examination by a medical practitioner it appoints, at its own cost. A claim needing no investigation is settled within fifteen days of the last document, and any investigation is completed within forty-five days of intimation. If the claim is declined because the illness does not meet its definition, the reasons and the definition relied on are given in writing.
