# Master Circular on Protection of Policyholders' Interests 2024 (DUMMY excerpt)

## 1. Applicability and principles

DUMMY: This fictitious master circular applies to every life insurer registered in India and to every distribution channel the insurer uses, including websites, mobile applications, call centres and automated conversational assistants operated by or for the insurer. It consolidates the policyholder protection requirements that apply from the pre-sale stage until the last claim under a policy is settled. An insurer shall treat every prospect and policyholder fairly, shall communicate in plain language, and shall offer the customer a choice of language where the channel allows it. Where any requirement of this circular conflicts with a product-specific approval, the stricter requirement applies. The board of every insurer shall approve a policy on the protection of policyholders' interests that implements this circular and shall review it at least once a year. The chapters that follow set out the obligations for solicitation, the customer information sheet, the proposal, the free-look period, policy servicing, claims, grievances and the handling of personal information.

## 2. Solicitation and sale

DUMMY: Chapter 2 governs how an insurer or its distributor approaches a prospect and recommends a product.

### 2.1 Disclosure of identity and channel

DUMMY: Before discussing any product, the insurer shall tell the prospect who is speaking on its behalf. A human distributor shall state their name, licence number and the insurer they represent. An automated assistant shall state at the start of the conversation that the prospect is interacting with an automated system operated by the insurer, and shall answer truthfully whenever the prospect asks whether they are speaking to a person. The prospect shall be told how to reach a licensed human advisor at any stage, and the request shall be honoured without requiring the prospect to repeat information already given.

### 2.2 Assessment of needs and suitability

DUMMY: No product shall be recommended unless the insurer has first assessed the prospect's insurance needs, financial position and ability to pay premiums for the full premium paying term. The assessment shall consider age, income, dependants, existing cover and outstanding liabilities. Where the information given is insufficient to assess suitability, the insurer shall say so and shall not recommend a product. A product that does not meet the documented need shall not be recommended merely because the prospect asks for a lower premium; the insurer shall instead explain the protection gap that results from any cheaper alternative.

### 2.3 Prohibited practices

DUMMY: An insurer or distributor shall not describe a non-guaranteed benefit as guaranteed, compare its product with another insurer's product in a misleading way, advise a policyholder to surrender or lapse an existing policy in order to buy a new one, or offer any rebate or inducement to buy a policy. Superlatives such as best, cheapest or risk-free shall not be used about any product. Any communication that promises tax benefits shall state that tax treatment depends on the law in force and may change.

## 3. Customer Information Sheet

DUMMY: Chapter 3 requires a customer information sheet for every product and sets out when it is given, what it contains and how its receipt is acknowledged.

### 3.1 Provision of the customer information sheet

DUMMY: A customer information sheet setting out the product's key features, benefits, exclusions and claim process shall be given to the prospect before the proposal is signed, in the language the prospect has chosen where a translation is available. On digital channels the sheet shall be displayed in full or made available through a link that opens without any login, and the prospect shall be given time to read it. The insurer shall record which version of the sheet was shown, identified by its version number or by a cryptographic hash of its content, so that the document shown can be reproduced during any later dispute.

### 3.2 Contents of the customer information sheet

DUMMY: The customer information sheet shall state, in the format specified by the Authority, the name and unique identification number of the product, the type of product, the sum assured and its basis, the premium and premium paying term, the policy term, every benefit payable, the exclusions and waiting periods, the free-look period, the grace period, the conditions for revival, the surrender value basis, the claim procedure with the documents required, and the contact details for grievances. Charges and guaranteed and non-guaranteed benefits shall be shown separately. The sheet shall not exceed two pages for a pure protection product.

### 3.3 Acknowledgment of the sheet

DUMMY: The prospect shall acknowledge receipt of the customer information sheet before the proposal is accepted. On digital channels the acknowledgment may be a structured action such as a checkbox or a button that names the document, captured with a timestamp. A free-text reply shall not be treated as an acknowledgment unless it unambiguously refers to the document. The insurer shall retain the acknowledgment together with the version or hash of the sheet that was acknowledged.

## 4. Proposal stage

DUMMY: Chapter 4 covers the proposal form, the disclosure of material facts by the proposer, and the time within which an insurer must decide on a proposal.

### 4.1 Proposal form and material facts

DUMMY: The proposal form shall ask clear questions about every fact that the insurer considers material to the acceptance of the risk, such as health history, tobacco use, occupation and existing insurance. The consequences of misstatement or non-disclosure of a material fact, including the provisions of section 45 of the Insurance Act, shall be explained in plain language next to the questions. Where the proposal is filled in through a conversational channel, the questions shall be presented in their approved wording and not paraphrased.

Provided that where the proposer asks for an explanation of a question, the insurer may give an approved explanation, but shall not suggest an answer.

### 4.2 Decision on the proposal

DUMMY: The insurer shall decide on a proposal and communicate the decision within fifteen days of receiving the completed proposal and all documents required. Where medical examination is needed, the period runs from the date the report is received. If the proposal is declined, the insurer shall refund the deposit within fifteen days of the decision, less only the cost of any medical examination actually incurred, and shall state the reason for the decision in writing where the law permits.

## 5. Free-look period

DUMMY: Chapter 5 gives every policyholder a period after receiving the policy document during which the policy may be returned.

### 5.1 Length of the free-look period

DUMMY: Every policyholder shall have a free-look period of thirty days from the date of receipt of the policy document, whether the policy is delivered in physical or electronic form, during which the policyholder may review the terms and return the policy if they disagree with any of them. The insurer shall state the free-look period prominently in the policy document, in the customer information sheet and in the welcome communication.

Provided that where the policy document is delivered electronically, the date of receipt is the date on which the electronic document is first made available to the policyholder.

Explanation.— A request to cancel within the free-look period does not require the policyholder to give any reason.

### 5.2 Refund on cancellation during free-look

DUMMY: On a free-look cancellation the insurer shall refund the premium paid within seven days of receiving the request, after deducting only the proportionate risk premium for the period of cover, stamp duty and the cost of any medical examination. For a unit-linked policy the refund shall also reflect the value of the units on the date of cancellation. No surrender charge or other penalty shall be levied on a free-look cancellation.

## 6. Policy servicing

DUMMY: Service requests such as a change of address, nomination, assignment, or the issue of a duplicate policy shall be acknowledged immediately and completed within seven working days of receipt of the request and the documents required. A loan against the policy, where the product allows one, shall be disbursed within seven days. The insurer shall send a premium due notice at least fifteen days before each premium due date and shall confirm every premium received. The insurer shall give the policyholder an annual statement of the policy status. Any request that cannot be completed within the stated time shall be escalated internally, and the policyholder shall be told the reason for the delay and the expected date of completion.

## 7. Claims

DUMMY: Chapter 7 sets the documents, timelines and duties that apply when a claim is made under a life insurance policy.

### 7.1 Intimation and documents for a death claim

DUMMY: A death claim may be intimated by the nominee, the legal heir or the assignee through any channel of the insurer, including branches, the website, email and the call centre. The insurer shall list the documents required at the first contact and shall not call for any document that is not relevant to the claim. For a claim within three years of the start or revival of the policy, the insurer may ask for additional documents needed to investigate the claim. The insurer shall appoint a claims officer as a single point of contact for the claimant.

### 7.2 Settlement timelines

DUMMY: A death claim that does not require investigation shall be settled within fifteen days of receipt of the last document. Where investigation is necessary, it shall be completed within forty-five days of intimation and the claim settled or repudiated within fifteen days after that. Maturity, survival and annuity benefits shall be paid on the due date, and the insurer shall ask for any required document at least sixty days in advance.

### 7.3 Repudiation and interest on delay

DUMMY: A claim shall be repudiated only on grounds that are stated in the policy contract, and the reasons shall be communicated in writing with the relevant clause and the evidence relied on. Every repudiation shall be reviewed by a claims review committee. If a claim is settled after the prescribed period, the insurer shall pay interest at two percent above the bank rate from the date of receipt of the last document until the date of payment.

## 8. Grievance redressal

DUMMY: Every insurer shall have a board-approved grievance redressal policy, a grievance redressal officer at its head office, and a system that records each complaint with a unique reference. A complaint shall be acknowledged within three days and resolved within fourteen days of receipt. The written reply shall inform the complainant that, if dissatisfied, they may escalate to the insurer's grievance redressal officer, register the complaint on the Authority's Bima Bharosa portal, or approach the Insurance Ombudsman. A complaint shall be treated as closed only when the complainant accepts the resolution or does not respond within eight weeks of the final reply.

## 9. Protection of personal information

DUMMY: The insurer shall collect only the personal information necessary for the purpose disclosed to the customer, shall obtain consent in accordance with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act where consent is the basis for processing, and shall keep the information confidential. Personal information shall not be shared with any person for marketing without separate consent, which the customer may withdraw at any time. The insurer shall implement reasonable security safeguards, shall report a personal data breach as the law requires, and shall erase personal information when the purpose is served and no law requires its retention.
