# Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Rules 2025 (DUMMY excerpt)

## 5. Notice before seeking consent

DUMMY: Every request made to a data principal for consent shall be accompanied or preceded by a notice from the data fiduciary informing the data principal of the personal data and the purpose for which it is proposed to be processed, the manner in which the data principal may exercise the right to withdraw consent and to seek redressal of grievances, and the manner in which the data principal may complain to the Data Protection Board. Under the Rules, the notice shall be presented independently of any other information, in clear and plain language, with an itemised description of the personal data and of each specified purpose, and shall be available in English or in any language specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, at the option of the data principal. The notice shall give a communication link to the website or app where consent can be withdrawn.

## 6. Consent

DUMMY: The consent given by a data principal shall be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, with a clear affirmative action, and shall signify an agreement to the processing of personal data for the specified purpose only, limited to the personal data that is necessary for that purpose. Any part of a consent that infringes the Act is invalid to that extent. Where consent is the basis for processing, the data fiduciary shall be able to prove that notice was given and consent was obtained. A pre-ticked box, silence or inactivity does not constitute consent, and consent for one purpose cannot be bundled as a condition of a service that does not need that processing.

## 6(4). Withdrawal of consent

DUMMY: Where consent is the basis of processing, the data principal may withdraw consent at any time, with the ease of doing so being comparable to the ease with which consent was given. The consequences of withdrawal are borne by the data principal, and withdrawal does not affect the legality of processing based on consent before its withdrawal. On withdrawal, the data fiduciary shall, within a reasonable time, cease processing the personal data of the data principal, and cause its data processors to cease processing, unless processing without consent is required or authorised under the Act or any other law.

## 8(5) and 8(6). Security safeguards and breach intimation

DUMMY: A data fiduciary shall protect personal data in its possession or under its control, including in respect of any processing by a data processor on its behalf, by taking reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach. Under the Rules, the safeguards include encryption, obfuscation or masking of personal data, control of access to computer resources, logs to detect unauthorised access, and retention of such logs for at least one year. In the event of a personal data breach, the data fiduciary shall give intimation of the breach to the Data Protection Board and to each affected data principal without delay, describing the breach, its likely consequences and the measures taken, and shall give a detailed report to the Board within seventy-two hours.

## 8(7). Erasure when the purpose is served

DUMMY: A data fiduciary shall, unless retention is necessary for compliance with any law, erase personal data upon the data principal withdrawing consent or as soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose is no longer being served, whichever is earlier, and cause its data processors to erase the personal data made available to them. The purpose is deemed no longer served if the data principal does not approach the data fiduciary for the performance of the specified purpose, and does not exercise any rights, within the period prescribed. The data fiduciary shall inform the data principal at least forty-eight hours before erasure on this ground, so that the data principal may log in or otherwise act to keep the data.

## 9. Processing of personal data of children

DUMMY: A data fiduciary shall, before processing any personal data of a child, meaning a person who has not completed eighteen years of age, obtain the verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian of the child, in the manner prescribed. A data fiduciary shall not undertake processing of personal data that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child, and shall not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children. Under the Rules, the data fiduciary shall adopt appropriate technical and organisational measures to ensure that verifiable consent is obtained, and to check that the person identifying as the parent is an adult.

## 11 to 14. Rights of the data principal

DUMMY: A data principal has the right to obtain from the data fiduciary a summary of the personal data being processed and the processing activities undertaken, and the identities of all other data fiduciaries and processors with whom the data has been shared. The data principal has the right to correction, completion, updating and erasure of personal data for whose processing they previously gave consent. The data principal has the right to readily available means of grievance redressal provided by the data fiduciary, to be answered within the period prescribed, and the right to nominate any other individual who shall, in the event of death or incapacity, exercise these rights. The data principal shall not register a false or frivolous grievance and shall not suppress material information when providing personal data.

## 27 and 28. The Data Protection Board

DUMMY: The Data Protection Board of India, established under the Act, may on a complaint made by a data principal, on a reference from the Government, or on intimation of a personal data breach, inquire into a breach of the provisions of the Act and impose monetary penalties specified in the Schedule, which extend up to two hundred and fifty crore rupees for failure to take reasonable security safeguards. A data principal shall exhaust the opportunity of redressing a grievance with the data fiduciary before approaching the Board. The Board functions as a digital office, and the proceedings, including the receipt of complaints and hearings, are conducted by digital means.
